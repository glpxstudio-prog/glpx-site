Holiday Party Photography in Orlando: How to Plan Photos for Your Company Event

By Gerson Lopez · GLPX Studio, Orlando FL

To get great photos of your company holiday party, book a photographer early, share your run of show, pick the 5 to 10 moments that matter most, and plan one setup that gives every guest a photo to take home, like a quick headshot station. The planning takes an hour. The photos work for your brand all year.

A holiday party is one of the few nights your whole team is relaxed, dressed up and in the same room. That makes it some of the most useful content a company gets all year: recruiting pages, LinkedIn posts, next year's invite and the culture slide in every pitch deck. I'm Gerson Lopez of GLPX Studio, and I cover corporate events across Orlando. Here is how to plan it so nothing important gets missed.

When should you book a holiday party photographer in Orlando?

Book as soon as your date and venue are confirmed. December weekends and the Thursdays and Fridays before the holidays tend to fill first, because so many companies want the same handful of nights.

If your party is in January, which some companies choose to avoid the December rush, you have a little more breathing room, but the same rule applies: lock in the photographer when you lock in the venue.

What should you send your photographer before the party?

The more I know ahead of time, the less I interrupt on the night. Send these a week out:

  • Run of show. Arrival, dinner, speeches, awards, games, toast, last call.
  • Must-have moments. The CEO's remarks, the award winners, the team photo, the raffle.
  • VIPs. Names and photos of the leaders, honorees or clients who must be covered.
  • Venue contact and load-in details. Parking, elevator access, where I can set up lights.
  • Where the photos will go. Internal recap, LinkedIn, careers page, press release or next year's sponsor deck.

That last one decides how I shoot. Photos for a careers page need wide, warm shots of people together. Photos for a press release need clean, well-lit moments at the podium.

What photos should you get at a company holiday party?

Use this as a starting shot list and cut what you do not need:

  1. Venue and decor before guests arrive
  2. Arrivals and greetings at the door
  3. Candid groups mid-conversation
  4. Leadership remarks and the toast
  5. Awards and recognition, with the handoff and a posed photo after
  6. Every team or department together
  7. One full company photo
  8. Food, drinks and the details your planner worked hard on
  9. Games, dancing and the moments people talk about on Monday
  10. A clean portrait of each honoree

Plan the full company photo early in the night, before anyone leaves and before the bar has done its job.

Should you add a headshot station?

If your team has outdated or mismatched LinkedIn photos, yes. A simple headshot setup in a side room lets staff step out for two minutes and come back with a professional photo. Everyone is already dressed up, so it is one of the most efficient ways to refresh the whole team at once.

It also gives every guest something personal to take home, which the guests remember more than the centerpieces. See team and corporate headshots for how a full team session works.

How much coverage do you need?

Most holiday parties fit one of two options:

Coverage Best for
Half day, up to 5 hours A dinner party, awards night or office celebration
Full day, up to 10 hours A daytime team event that rolls into an evening party, or several rooms or locations

Edits are added per image, so you choose how many finals you need. Full-day coverage includes travel within 1 hour of Orlando, which covers venues from Downtown Orlando and Lake Nona to Kissimmee and Winter Park. You can also add on-site team headshots or a short event recap video.

Do you need photos and video?

If you want something to post the next week, a short recap video gets more reach on Instagram and LinkedIn than a photo album. If the goal is a careers page or an annual report, photos do more of the work. Many companies book both and use the video for social and the photos everywhere else.

How do you make guests comfortable on camera?

Tell people a photographer will be there. A line in the invite is enough. On the night, I move with the room instead of stopping it, and I ask before posing groups. People relax when they realize they will not be pulled away from the party for long.

If some guests prefer not to be photographed, let me know who or give them a simple signal, like a different color lanyard. It takes care of itself.

The practical takeaway

A week before your party, send your photographer three things: the run of show, the 5 to 10 must-have moments, and where the photos will be used. With those in hand, you get coverage that works for your brand long after the decorations come down.
