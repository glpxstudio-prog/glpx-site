# How to Prepare for a Branding Photoshoot: A 10-Step Plan
date: Nov 16, 2026

To prepare for a branding photoshoot, decide where the photos will be used, build a shot list of the exact images you need, plan 2 to 4 outfits that match your brand colors, and gather props that show what you do. The more specific your plan, the more usable photos you walk away with.

Most people walk into a branding session thinking about how they will look. The people who get the best results walk in knowing what they need. This is the same 10-step prep I use with entrepreneurs, consultants and creative brands across Orlando.

## Step 1: Why are you doing this shoot?

Start with one clear goal. Launching a new website? Rebranding? Filling three months of Instagram? Pitching to corporate clients? Each goal needs different images.

Write it as one sentence, for example: "I need photos for my new website and 8 weeks of social posts before my January launch."

## Step 2: Where will the photos live?

List every place the images will appear: website hero, About page, Instagram grid, Stories, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, email headers, a speaker page or ads.

This matters because each spot needs a different shape. Website banners are wide. Stories are tall. Profile photos are square. When I know this ahead of time, I shoot with room for text and every crop you need.

## Step 3: Who is your ideal client?

Picture the person you want to book you. What do they need to feel when they land on your website? Trust? Excitement? Calm? Luxury? That feeling guides the lighting, the locations and the expressions we go after.

## Step 4: Build a moodboard

Save 10 to 20 images that match the look you want: lighting, colors, poses, settings. Pinterest works well. Include a few images you do not like, too. Knowing what to avoid is just as useful.

After our strategy call, I turn your moodboard into the creative direction for the shoot. You bring the inspiration, and we refine it into a plan built for your brand.

## Step 5: Write your shot list

A shot list is a checklist of the exact images you need. It keeps the session focused and makes sure nothing important is missed. A starter list for most service businesses:

- Classic portrait, smiling, looking at camera
- Confident portrait, arms crossed or seated
- Working shot: at your laptop, with a client or with your tools
- Close-up details: hands, product, notebook, coffee
- Wide shot with space for website text
- Vertical frames for Stories and Reels covers
- Lifestyle moments: walking, laughing, thinking

## Step 6: Plan 2 to 4 outfits

My Starter Branding package covers 2 outfits and Signature Branding covers 4, so plan your wardrobe around that. Choose outfits that match your brand palette and what your clients expect. A realtor might pair one polished blazer look with one relaxed weekend look. A fitness coach might bring training gear and one off-duty outfit.

Stick to solids or simple textures, steam everything the night before and bring it on hangers. My guide on [what to wear for headshots](blog/what-to-wear-for-headshots-orlando/) covers colors and fit in more detail.

## Step 7: Gather props that tell your story

Props should show what you actually do: a stylist's shears, a consultant's notebook, a baker's tools, your product packaging, a microphone for a podcaster. Bring a branded mug, a laptop with a clean screen, or printed materials with your logo.

Leave out props that do not connect to your business. If it would confuse a new client, leave it at home.

## Step 8: Choose the right location

I shoot in professional rented studios in Orlando and on location. A studio gives you controlled light and clean backdrops. Your own office, a Winter Park storefront or a Downtown Orlando street gives context and personality.

Many clients mix both: a studio portrait set for the website, then a short on-location set for social content. For ideas, see [the best places for branding photos in Orlando](blog/best-places-for-branding-photos-in-orlando/).

## Step 9: Book hair, makeup and content add-ons early

If you want a hair and makeup artist, book them with the session. This is also the time to decide on behind-the-scenes stills or short Reels from the same day. Capturing photo and video together saves you from planning a second production.

## Step 10: Get ready for shoot day

- Sleep well and drink water the day before
- Get nails and grooming done 1 to 2 days ahead
- Charge your phone and laptop if they appear in shots
- Arrive 15 minutes early
- Bring a snack, water and your shot list
- Keep the rest of your day light so you are not rushing out the door

## The practical takeaway

Before your shoot, finish three things: your one-sentence goal, your list of where the photos will live, and your shot list. With those ready, every frame gets planned around results instead of guesswork.

## Questions

### How far in advance should I book a branding photoshoot?

Two to four weeks gives enough time for a strategy call, creative direction and wardrobe planning. Book earlier for launches and the holiday season.

### How long does a branding photoshoot take?

Starter Branding is a 2-hour shoot and Signature Branding is 3 hours. Half-day sessions run up to 5 hours.

### Do I need to know how to pose?

No. I guide you through every pose and expression on set.

### What should I bring to a branding shoot?

Your outfits on hangers, props that represent your business, your shot list, water and a snack. A lint roller helps too.

### Can I bring my team or a friend?

Yes. Guests and team members are welcome on set. Everyone who comes along is asked to keep the session professional and respectful so you can stay focused in front of the camera.
