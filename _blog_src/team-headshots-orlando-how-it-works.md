# Team Headshots in Orlando: How It Works
date: Nov 09, 2026

The usual way a company ends up with team headshots is this. Someone takes photos at the holiday party. Two people send selfies. One person sends a photo from a wedding. Three new hires have nothing. The website looks like a ransom note.

The fix is one session where everyone is photographed the same way. Here is what that actually involves.

## Consistency is the product

With team headshots, matching matters more than any single photo being perfect.

Same light, same background, same distance, same crop, same color. When those line up, your leadership page looks like a company. When they do not, it looks like a collage, and people read that as sloppy whether they mean to or not.

This is also why adding one person later is a real consideration, not an afterthought. I keep the setup notes for every team session so a new hire shot six months on still drops into the grid without standing out.

## On site or in a studio

**On site at your office** is usually the right answer for groups. I bring lights and a backdrop and set up in a conference room or any space with about ten feet of clear wall. People step out for a few minutes and go back to work. Nobody loses half a day to travel.

**A studio in Orlando** is better when your office has no usable space, when you want a more polished look, or when the group is small enough to travel.

## The schedule math

Plan on five to seven minutes per person once the lights are up. That covers walking in, a posture reset, two or three expressions and a look at the back of the camera.

Twenty people fits in a morning. Forty is a full day or two half days. Setup takes about forty five minutes before the first person sits down, so the first slot should not be the CEO who has a hard out at nine.

Send me the headcount and I will build the running order. Group people by department so nobody is hunting for the room.

## What your team needs to know beforehand

Send one short note a week out. It makes a bigger difference than anything I do on the day.

- **Solid colors, no busy patterns.** A jacket or blazer if that fits your culture.
- **Bring a second top** for anyone who wants options.
- **Haircuts a week early,** not the night before.
- **Nothing brand new,** so collars sit flat.
- **Where and when,** with the five minute slot each person is in.

## Nobody knows how to pose

This is the part companies underestimate. Most people have never been photographed properly and they tense up the second the camera comes out.

I direct the whole thing. Where the feet go, what the shoulders do, where the chin sits, where to look. Nobody has to figure it out. The people who tell me they hate having their picture taken are usually the ones who end up with the best frame, because they stop performing and just stand there.

## What you get back

One retouched final per person as standard, delivered in a square crop for the website, a wider crop for presentations and a circle safe version for profile photos.

Retouching keeps skin texture. Stray hairs, a distracting shadow, a crooked collar, all fixed. Nobody looks airbrushed. The point is that your team looks like themselves on a good day.

Files arrive named by person so your web team is not guessing.
