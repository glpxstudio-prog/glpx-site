# Social Video for Orlando Brands
date: Nov 09, 2026

Most businesses do not need a brand film. They need forty seconds that makes someone stop scrolling, and they need it often enough that the algorithm notices.

Those are different jobs and they cost different money. Here is how I think about video for Orlando businesses.

## The two kinds of video worth paying for

**Content video.** Short, vertical, a lot of it. Reels, TikToks, Shorts. Shot fast, cut fast, posted weekly. The goal is volume and consistency, not polish. A phone with good light beats a cinema camera with bad light every time, and nobody scrolling can tell which one you used.

**Anchor video.** One piece that has to carry weight. The homepage video, the explainer that sits in your sales email, the recap that sells next year's event. Slower to make, shot properly, lit properly, and worth it because it works for a year.

Deciding which one you actually need is the whole conversation. Most businesses ask for the second and need the first.

## Shoot photo and video on the same day

This is the easiest win available and most people miss it.

If I am already in your space with lights up and you already look good, adding video costs a fraction of what a separate shoot would. Same wardrobe, same setup, same hour. You walk away with headshots, branding stills and a batch of vertical clips.

Doing them as two shoots means two days of your time, two setups and two invoices. Doing them together is one planned session. If you are booking [branding photography](branding.html), ask about adding clips before the day, not after.

## What makes social video work

- **The first second.** Not the first three. The first frame has to show something or say something. No logo intros, no slow fades, no "hey guys".
- **Vertical, framed for the interface.** Captions and buttons eat the bottom third of the screen. Anything important sits in the middle.
- **Captions burned in.** Most people watch muted.
- **Shot in batches.** Ten clips from one session beats one clip from ten sessions, and it is how you actually post weekly without hating your life.
- **Short.** If it can be twenty seconds, it should not be sixty.

## Event video

Event coverage is its own thing. A recap that just shows rooms and name badges does nothing. A recap that tells the story of the day sells tickets to the next one.

That means planning around your run of show before the day. Which speaker matters, where the energy peaks, which sponsor needs to be visible, what the one line is that the whole film hangs on. Then we shoot for that film instead of filming everything and hoping.

Same for interviews. Three good vendor or attendee interviews cut into a recap do more than twenty minutes of b roll.

## What I shoot with

Sony FX3 for anything that needs to look properly made, graded for a clean, warm look rather than something flat and gray. Phone footage when the brief is volume and speed, because the best camera for weekly content is the one that does not add a day of work.

For fast turnarounds I work with an outside editor, which is how a recap can land while the event is still worth posting about.
