# How Often Should You Update Your Brand Photos?
date: Nov 16, 2026

Most businesses should update their brand photos every 12 to 24 months, and sooner if something visible changes: your look, your logo, your location, your team or the services you sell. If your photos no longer match what a client sees when they meet you, it is time for new ones.

Brand photos have a shelf life. The same portrait that looked great on your website two years ago can quietly start working against you: a different haircut, a team that has doubled, a rebrand with new colors, or a feed that has shown the same five images for a year. I'm Gerson Lopez of GLPX Studio, and here is how to tell when your photos need a refresh, and how to plan one so the next set lasts longer.

## What are the signs your brand photos are outdated?

Look at your website and Instagram with fresh eyes. You need new photos if:

- **You look different.** New haircut, new glasses, a big change in style. Clients should recognize you when you show up.
- **Your brand changed.** New logo, colors or name. Old photos with the old palette make the rebrand feel unfinished.
- **Your team changed.** People on your About page who no longer work there, or new hires with no photo.
- **You offer new services.** Your photos show the business you were, not the business you are now.
- **You moved or upgraded your space.** A new office, salon or storefront is a reason to show it off.
- **You keep reusing the same few images.** Followers notice, and so does the algorithm.
- **Your photos are phone selfies.** They got you started. They are not doing you favors next to competitors with professional images.

## How often should different businesses refresh their photos?

How often depends on how much you post and how fast your business changes.

| Business type | Suggested refresh |
| --- | --- |
| Corporate headshot for LinkedIn | Every 1 to 2 years, or after a big change in appearance |
| Service business website (consultant, realtor, coach) | Every 12 to 18 months |
| Brands that post on Instagram several times a week | Every 3 to 6 months, or a planned content day each quarter |
| Product brands | Every new product launch or season |
| Teams | Whenever several new people join |

These are starting points, not rules. If you post often, you will run out of fresh images long before your website photos look dated.

## Why do brand photos matter so much?

Your photos are often the first impression a potential client gets, usually before they read a word. Consistent, current images tell people you are active, professional and paying attention to details. Outdated ones raise a quiet question: is this business still current?

Fresh photos also give you something new to post, which helps you stay consistent on social media without repeating yourself.

## How do you make your next brand photos last longer?

A few choices on shoot day stretch the life of your images:

1. **Plan for every shape.** Shoot horizontal, vertical and square versions so the same session covers your website, Stories and ads.
2. **Choose timeless wardrobe.** Solid colors and classic cuts age better than trends. See [what to wear for headshots](blog/what-to-wear-for-headshots-orlando/).
3. **Shoot variety.** Portraits, working shots, details and lifestyle moments give you months of different posts.
4. **Capture content at the same time.** Behind-the-scenes stills and short Reels from the same day add weeks of social content.
5. **Leave room for text.** Images with empty space work as website banners and ad backgrounds.

## Should you do one big shoot or several small ones?

If you post often, a regular content session every quarter keeps your feed fresh and your look consistent. If you mostly need website and profile images, one longer session every year or so is usually enough.

## The practical takeaway

Open your website, your Google Business Profile and your last 12 Instagram posts side by side. If any photo shows a look, a space, a team or a brand you have moved past, put a refresh on the calendar.

Every 1 to 2 years, or sooner if your appearance changes. Your photo should look like the person who shows up to the meeting.

Yes, as long as the lighting, colors and style feel consistent. A planned refresh can match your existing look.
